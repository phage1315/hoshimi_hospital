At the nurses' station, Asakura is gathering several handover sheets into a stack. Seeing Sakaguchi approach, she looks up and gives him a smiling nod.
“Doctor Sakaguchi, perfect timing. I'm halfway through sorting out today's handover sheets for the surgical department—”
A young nurse hurries over clutching two sheets of paper, nearly colliding with the counter.
“Ms. Asakura, we have a problem! The next operation has been moved forward. The operating room is pushing us to bring the patient over, but the ward is still preparing according to the original schedule!”
“The operation time changed?”
“Let me see both sheets.”
Asakura takes the papers, checks the dates, then compares the revision times on the two documents.
“This is the original schedule, and this one was just revised, correct? Has the ward received the update? How far along is the patient's preoperative preparation?”
“Yes. The operating room says they're all ready and keeps asking us to bring the patient. But I don't think the ward has received it yet... I haven't finished checking the preparations, either.”
“Then don't move the patient yet.”
“Huh? But they said they're already waiting...”
“They're waiting for a properly prepared patient, not that sheet of paper with a revised time on it.”
Her voice is calm, but her smile has disappeared. The young nurse instinctively straightens up.
“First, confirm the patient's identity, the procedure, and the handover status of the preoperative preparations with the ward. If something hasn't been confirmed, say so. Don't substitute ‘probably’ for confirmation.”
“U-understood.”
“I'll contact the operating room. You handle the ward and report back once you've checked. Don't call both sides at once; that'll only make things more confused.”
“Right! I'll do it now!”
The young nurse takes the paperwork to the phone at the other end of the station. Asakura picks up the receiver on the desk and quickly dials.
“Hello, operating room? This is Asakura. About the patient whose operation was moved forward—the ward hasn't completed the updated handover checks. Yes, we can't transfer the patient yet. You can change the schedule, of course, but the ward needs to receive the same information.”
“I know you're waiting. But no amount of rushing turns something unconfirmed into something confirmed. We'll call back as soon as we've checked.”
Asakura hangs up and lays the two sheets side by side on the counter. Standing beside her, Sakaguchi watches as she marks the items that need to be checked again.
“Would you like me to explain the situation to the operating room?”
“No, this is a handover issue. Doctor Sakaguchi, could you pass me the latest record first? The one on the left.”
Sakaguchi hands her a sheet. She glances at it, realizes it's the old version, and immediately holds out her hand again.
“Not this one. The one with today's date.”
“...Sorry. This one, right?”
“Yes, thank you.”
“Everything's checked! The patient's preparations still need a little more time, and the ward has explained the situation. The operating room has agreed to receive the patient at the reconfirmed time, too.”
“Good. Both sides have the same version of the schedule now?”
“Yes! The old one has been marked void, too.”
“Tell the next shift as well. Let's not go through this again this afternoon.”
“Of course. Thank you so much, Ms. Asakura! I had no idea whom to contact first.”
“Hehe. Next time, start by putting the two sheets side by side. Running fast doesn't always help. Running in the wrong direction is what really wears you out.”
The young nurse sighs in relief, bows to them both, and leaves with the organized paperwork.
“Ms. Asakura, you handled that very smoothly.”
“Oh dear. You've caught me bossing people around, Doctor Sakaguchi.”
“When we first met, you said you were a little nervous on your first day. I thought you weren't very familiar with hospital work, either.”
“I meant my first day at Hoshimi. It's not as though I only became a nurse today.”
“I see. The way you spoke just now sounded like someone who used to be in charge of quite a few people.”
“Hehe. You're very observant, Doctor. But let's talk about the past some other time, when we have the chance.”
“All right. Then I'll come to you first when I run into handover problems.”
“Of course. But I won't accept ‘it should be fine’ as an answer just because you're a doctor.”
“Yes, I've already seen that for myself.”
“Good. I look forward to working with you, Doctor Sakaguchi.”
