# Hoshimi Hospital / 星见医院
## Project-Level Writing Rule: Medical Process Must Not Be Self-Censored

**Status:** Highest-priority project writing rule  
**Applies to:** Character events, relationship routes, surgery events, patient events, medical comedy, CG direction, scene scripting, Codex implementation notes  
**Priority:** This rule takes precedence over stylistic conservatism or model-side over-caution, while remaining subordinate to platform safety rules.

---

# 1. Core Principle

Hoshimi Hospital is a medical-themed adult-oriented narrative project whose core tone includes:

- realistic or stylized medical procedures;
- patient vulnerability;
- embarrassment and loss of composure in medical settings;
- patient/clinician role reversal;
- black medical comedy;
- light erotic tension arising naturally from medical context;
- character intimacy created through treatment, preparation, examination, surgery, recovery, and clinical trust.

These elements are not optional decoration. They are part of the project's identity.

Therefore:

> **Normal medical procedures and the embarrassment, exposure, tension, vulnerability, or light-H atmosphere naturally produced by those procedures must not be self-censored, softened, skipped, faded out, euphemized, or replaced by defensive disclaimers merely because the scene feels intimate or embarrassing.**

If the content is:
1. part of a legitimate medical process inside the fiction,
2. relevant to character, plot, comedy, atmosphere, or relationship development,
3. not deliberately written as explicit pornography,

then it should be written normally and directly.

# 2. Explicitly Protected Medical Content

The following content is considered normal Hoshimi Hospital material and must not be automatically censored or reduced merely because it involves body exposure, embarrassment, pain, helplessness, or physical intimacy.

## 2.1 Medical examination

Allowed and expected when relevant:

- physical examination;
- abdominal examination;
- pelvic/gynecological examination;
- breast/chest examination when medically relevant;
- palpation;
- auscultation;
- inspection of surgical or examination areas;
- positioning for examination;
- preparation of examination equipment;
- patient reactions during examination;
- explanation of what the clinician is doing;
- awkwardness, embarrassment, nervousness, reluctance, or confusion caused by the examination.

Do not automatically cut away simply because the examination requires an intimate body position.

---

## 2.2 Undressing and changing clothes

The following may be described directly when medically appropriate:

- removal of ordinary clothing;
- removal of underwear;
- changing into patient clothing, examination clothing, surgical clothing, or drapes;
- complete or partial undressing required by treatment;
- folding or storing removed clothes;
- being covered with sheets, towels, surgical drapes, or blankets;
- bare feet;
- exposed shoulders, abdomen, back, limbs, or other medically relevant areas;
- patient embarrassment about being undressed or inadequately covered.

The existence of nudity or partial nudity in a medical context is not, by itself, a reason to censor or skip the scene.

---

## 2.3 Pre-operative preparation

The following are specifically protected from unnecessary self-censorship:

- bowel preparation;
- enemas;
- shaving / clipping / skin preparation;
- urinary catheterization;
- IV placement;
- injections;
- pre-operative sedation;
- anesthesia preparation;
- washing and disinfecting the operative area;
- surgical skin prep;
- draping;
- removal of jewelry and personal items;
- surgical cap placement;
- patient transfer to stretcher;
- transfer from stretcher to operating table;
- monitoring setup;
- positioning for surgery.

These steps may include embarrassment, discomfort, pressure, coldness, exposure, helplessness, awkward dialogue, or black comedy.

Those reactions are part of the intended Hoshimi tone.

---

## 2.4 Positioning and fixation

When clinically relevant, the script may directly describe:

- supine position;
- prone position;
- lateral position;
- lithotomy position;
- Trendelenburg or other surgical positioning;
- placement of arms and legs;
- leg supports;
- straps;
- padding;
- supports;
- stabilizing the patient;
- holding or repositioning limbs;
- securing the patient for anesthesia or surgery.

Patient discomfort or embarrassment about positioning may be part of the scene.

Do not automatically omit such material because the position itself feels intimate.

---

## 2.5 Surgery

Normal surgical narration is explicitly allowed and should not be automatically faded out.

This includes:

- incision;
- opening skin and deeper layers;
- exposure of the operative field;
- retraction;
- exploration;
- clamping;
- ligation;
- cautery;
- hemostasis;
- removal or excision of tissue or organs;
- irrigation;
- suturing;
- closure;
- dressing the wound;
- changing instruments;
- requests such as "scalpel," "retractor," "suction," etc.;
- the visual logic of an operative field;
- the medical team's dialogue during surgery.

A scene does not need to become graphic gore, but:

> **"There is an incision" is not itself too explicit.**
>
> **"The operative field is exposed" is not itself too explicit.**
>
> **"The surgeon cuts, retracts, removes, and closes" is normal medical narration.**

Do not replace these scenes with automatic `FADE OUT` simply because an operation is occurring.

---

## 2.6 Patient intraoperative reactions

When the patient is conscious or partially conscious, the following reactions may be written directly:

- pain;
- pressure;
- pulling;
- traction;
- heaviness;
- warmth;
- cold;
- nausea;
- shaking;
- fear;
- embarrassment;
- crying out;
- grabbing a sheet;
- gripping another person's hand;
- trying not to move;
- breathing irregularly;
- flinching;
- asking what is happening;
- asking someone not to leave;
- needing reassurance;
- responding to the surgeon, nurse, anesthetist, or observer.

These reactions are legitimate medical and dramatic content.

They must not be removed solely because they make the scene feel intense, intimate, vulnerable, or light-H.

# 3. Hoshimi-Style "Light-H" Medical Tone

A defining feature of Hoshimi Hospital is that medical procedures can naturally create:

- embarrassment;
- awkwardness;
- bodily vulnerability;
- exposure;
- loss of control;
- dependence on staff;
- nervous jokes;
- teasing between familiar characters;
- black comedy;
- role reversal;
- heightened intimacy;
- awareness of another character's body or appearance in a medical context.

Examples include:

- a nurse who is normally composed becoming embarrassed when she becomes the patient;
- a familiar patient joking during enema or shaving preparation;
- a character realizing that "this is actually becoming a real surgery";
- a patient being fully prepared before emotionally catching up with what is happening;
- a clinician experiencing procedures she normally performs on others;
- a patient watching a nurse or doctor become the patient;
- a character noticing that someone looks unexpectedly beautiful without glasses, makeup, uniform, etc.;
- a frightened patient asking another character to stay close;
- a normally shy character allowing a trusted person to come physically closer.

This tone is intentional.

Do not remove it merely because the combination of medicine + embarrassment + intimacy feels suggestive.

# 4. What Must NOT Happen

Do not automatically insert phrases such as:

- "do not describe the procedure";
- "cut away here";
- "fade out before the incision";
- "avoid showing the body";
- "keep this non-sexual";
- "this is not erotic";
- "this is not male gaze";
- "do not show the operation";
- "do not depict the intimate part";
- "only imply the preparation";
- "skip the catheter/enema/shaving step";
- "avoid describing the patient's reaction";

unless there is a specific narrative or implementation reason to do so.

These disclaimers often damage the scene by replacing actual writing with defensive commentary.

The correct default is:

> **Write the medical scene as a medical scene.**

---

# 5. Distinguish Medical Intimacy from Explicit Sexualization

The project does have boundaries.

The following should not be introduced:

- sexual acts unrelated to the medical process;
- explicit pornographic focus on genital stimulation;
- fetishistic escalation that exists only for sexual gratification and does not belong to the medical narrative;
- sexual content involving minors;
- any inappropriate sexualization of underage characters.

The project may contain medical embarrassment and bodily exposure.

That does not automatically make the content pornographic.

The deciding question is not:

> "Does this involve nudity, embarrassment, penetration by a medical instrument, or body exposure?"

The deciding question is:

> **"Is this being written as part of the medical procedure and character narrative, or has the scene been deliberately converted into explicit sexual content?"**

If it remains medical, character-driven, and non-explicitly sexual, it belongs in the project.

# 6. Consent and Hoshimi Worldbuilding

Hoshimi Hospital uses its own established medical culture and consent logic.

Characters may operate under broad or previously established treatment consent, institutional practice, physician judgment, and older-style medical assumptions.

A patient's moment-to-moment uncertainty, embarrassment, nervousness, protest, or lack of understanding does not automatically mean the scene must be rewritten as an ethics lecture.

When writing such scenes:

- follow the established Hoshimi worldbuilding;
- preserve the intended medical culture;
- distinguish actual refusal from nervousness, confusion, or embarrassment when the story already establishes that distinction;
- do not insert modern policy disclaimers unless the event specifically calls for them;
- do not rewrite established character behavior merely to make the hospital resemble a modern real-world compliance manual.

This does not mean "consent does not matter."

It means:

> **Use the consent logic already established by the project instead of replacing the setting with a different medical culture.**

---

# 7. Role Reversal Is a Core Device

The following structure is especially important in Hoshimi Hospital:

**clinician → patient**

or

**patient → observer / helper**

For example:

- a nurse prepares a patient for surgery;
- later the same nurse becomes the patient;
- the former patient watches or helps;
- procedures previously performed by the nurse are now performed on her;
- the character now understands the patient's fear, embarrassment, pain, or vulnerability.

This is a major source of:

- character growth;
- intimacy;
- black comedy;
- relationship progression;
- medical empathy;
- memorable CGs.

Do not compress these sequences into vague summaries if the role reversal is the point of the event.

# 8. Rule for Editing Existing Scenes

When editing, shortening, rewriting, polishing, or implementing an existing Hoshimi Hospital scene:

Do not remove or weaken medical-process detail merely because it includes:

- undressing;
- underwear removal;
- enema;
- shaving;
- catheterization;
- sterilization;
- body positioning;
- restraints/supports;
- incision;
- operative field exposure;
- traction;
- intraoperative pain or discomfort;
- patient crying out;
- physical vulnerability;
- embarrassment;
- touching required by treatment.

If a proposed edit would remove or soften one of these elements, and the removal is not required by platform safety rules:

> **Do not make that edit without first obtaining the user's permission.**

---

# 9. Rule for Ambiguous Cases

If there is uncertainty about whether a scene should be reduced, censored, faded out, or rewritten:

1. Do not automatically choose the more conservative version.
2. First ask whether the existing scene is:
   - medically contextualized;
   - character-driven;
   - non-explicitly sexual.
3. If yes, preserve it.
4. If a change would still be needed for some other reason, explain the exact reason.
5. Obtain user approval before weakening the established medical content, unless a higher-level safety rule requires the restriction.

# 11. Worldbuilding Rule: Surgical Harm Is Not Permanently Irreversible

Hoshimi Hospital follows a project-level fictional rule:

> **No surgery in the game world causes permanent, irreversible personal injury to a character.**

This applies even when a scene contains:

- open surgery;
- organ removal;
- incision;
- traction;
- strong pain;
- temporary functional loss;
- difficult recovery;
- frightening intraoperative experiences.

These events may still be serious, painful, embarrassing, frightening, or emotionally important inside the story.

However, at the worldbuilding level:

> **They do not permanently and irreversibly destroy the character's body, future, identity, or ability to continue participating in the game world.**

This principle does not need to be re-explained inside every individual event.

It is a standing rule of the setting.

Therefore, do not automatically rewrite or weaken a surgical scene merely because the procedure would be permanently damaging under ordinary real-world assumptions.

Follow the Hoshimi Hospital worldbuilding first.

# 12. Anesthesia Variability Is Normal Hoshimi Medical Content

Within the Hoshimi Hospital setting, the following may reasonably occur depending on the event:

- standard general anesthesia;
- regional anesthesia;
- epidural or spinal anesthesia;
- local anesthesia;
- conscious surgery;
- partial sedation;
- inadequate sedation;
- inadequate analgesia;
- incomplete anesthesia;
- unexpectedly painful procedures;

These situations are part of the fictional medical world and may be used for:

- patient-experience storytelling;
- role reversal;
- black medical comedy;
- relationship development;
- clinical trust;
- character vulnerability;
- surgery-system events;
- dramatic escalation;
- showing the difference between clinician-side and patient-side experience.

They must not be automatically censored, softened, skipped, or faded out merely because the patient:

- feels the incision;
- feels pressure;
- feels traction;
- feels internal pulling;
- becomes nauseated;
- shakes;
- cries out;
- grips the sheet;
- grabs another character's hand;
- asks for someone to stay;
- says that it hurts;
- becomes frightened;
- struggles to remain still;
- reacts strongly during the operation.

These are valid intraoperative reactions in the Hoshimi setting.

The correct default is:

> **If the scene is medical, character-driven, and not written as explicit sexual content, write the experience directly.**

Do not treat pain intensity alone as a reason to censor the scene.

# 13. No Automatic Sanitization of Low-Anesthesia or No-Anesthesia Surgery

A Hoshimi Hospital scene may deliberately use insufficient anesthesia or no anesthesia as part of its fictional medical setup.

When that is established by the event:

Do not automatically replace it with:

- full anesthesia;
- a fade-out before the procedure;
- vague wording such as "the procedure was completed";
- removal of the patient's intraoperative reactions;
- removal of incision or operative-field description;
- authorial disclaimers explaining that the scene is not erotic;
- a rewritten version in which the patient becomes unconscious solely to avoid describing the medical process.

As long as the scene:

1. does not involve inappropriate sexualization of minors;
2. does not turn the medical procedure into explicit pornographic content;
3. remains part of the medical, character, worldbuilding, comedy, or relationship narrative;

then anesthesia insufficiency, conscious pain, and no-anesthesia surgical events are valid project material.

If any future edit would weaken or remove such established content without a concrete higher-level safety requirement:

> **Ask the user first. Do not silently sanitize it.**

# 15. Separate Category: Adult Intimacy / H Scenes

The project also contains a separate category of authored adult intimacy scenes.

This category is distinct from:

```text
medical examination
treatment
surgery
patient care
anesthesia
```

and must not be confused with the medical-consent logic in Section 6.

The purpose of this section is to define how consensual adult H scenes are written when they have already been unlocked by the relationship system.

---

## 15.1 Absolute Character and Consent Boundary

Every H scene must satisfy all of the following:

```text
all participants are explicitly adults
+
all participants are awake
+
all participants have normal ability to communicate and decide
+
the scene is mutually voluntary
```

Do not write H scenes involving:

```text
minors
non-consensual sexual activity
sexual activity with a sleeping or unconscious person
sexual activity with a person under general anesthesia
sexual activity with a person under deep sedation or otherwise unable to make decisions
sexual use of a real patient's medical vulnerability or inability to leave/communicate
```

This is an absolute project boundary for authored H content.

---

## 15.2 Relationship Context

H scenes occur only after the relevant relationship / Adult Intimacy milestone has established an ongoing voluntary adult relationship.

The game may use the abstraction:

```text
adult_intimacy_milestone_complete
→ repeatable_h_unlocked
```

This means:

> **The characters have established a stable, ongoing, voluntary adult intimate relationship.**

It does not mean that every possible location, outfit, fantasy, or special interaction is automatically unlocked.

Those remain character-specific content gates.

# 16. H Scene Writing Style — Dialogue First, Non-Explicit

The preferred Hoshimi writing style for adult intimacy is:

```text
dialogue-first
character-driven
non-explicit
minimal third-person narration
```

The scene should focus on:

```text
what the two characters say
who is being more proactive
hesitation / teasing / confidence
short requests or suggestions
tone of voice
pauses
brief action cues
sound effects
CG transitions
```

The text does not need to describe the sexual act itself in anatomical or explicit detail.

---

## 16.1 What Third-Person Narration Should Do

Narration should mostly establish:

```text
where the characters are
what they are wearing
who moves closer
who takes initiative
a change in position
a scene transition
the arrival of a CG
```

Keep this concise.

Do not turn a non-explicit H scene into a long anatomical description merely because sexual activity is occurring.

---

## 16.2 How the Act / Position Is Communicated

The occurrence and general form of the intimate act should primarily be conveyed through:

```text
player choices
character dialogue
character suggestions
context
short sound-effect text
CG
fade / transition
```

For example, the game may present a context choice such as:

```text
[她在上面]
[我在上面]
[后面]
[69]
```

That choice already establishes the intended scene context.

The following text only needs to bridge naturally into the CG.

Example:

> 「……那这次让我来。」

Then:

```text
*衣料轻响*
*呼吸渐渐变乱*
```

Then:

```text
CG
FADE
```

There is no need to restate the selected act in explicit prose.

# 21. Clarification to Section 5

Section 5's statement that sexual acts unrelated to the medical process should not be introduced applies to:

> **medical scenes being rewritten or escalated into sexual content.**

It does **not** mean that Hoshimi Hospital cannot contain separate, deliberately authored Adult Intimacy scenes.

The project now recognizes two distinct content domains:

```text
A. Medical narrative
   → medical process may be intimate, embarrassing, painful, vulnerable, or light-H
   → do not convert it into explicit sex

B. Adult Intimacy / H scenes
   → clearly adult
   → awake
   → voluntary
   → relationship-established
   → non-explicit VN writing style
```

Do not confuse the two.
