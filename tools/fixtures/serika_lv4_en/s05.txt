The following morning, Sakaguchi came to the ward. Before he reached the door, he saw two people standing beside it.
Serika stood very straight, though she held no chart. Miyuki Asakura stood beside her, quietly confirming visiting hours with a nurse.
“Morning. You're both here.”
“Dr. Sakaguchi. The nurse says she's awake and can speak a little.”
“…I'd like to see her.”
“You may. But don't tire her out. Anything else can wait for another day.”
The three entered the room. Nanami rested partly upright against her pillows. Hearing footsteps, she slowly turned her face.
“…Doctor?”
“I'm Sakaguchi. I operated on you in the early hours yesterday. This is Hoshimi Hospital. You're safe now.”
Nanami blinked. Her gaze passed Sakaguchi and settled on Serika's face.
“…Dr.… Serika?”
“…Nanami.”
“Is it really… you?”
“It's me.”
Nanami's eyes immediately reddened. She tried to push herself up, then stopped when it pulled at her wound.
“Don't get up. Your abdominal incision needs time to heal.”
“I'm sorry… I thought… I'd never…”
Asakura approached the bed and gently adjusted the pillows so Nanami could rest back comfortably.
“You don't need to finish that now. Look, we're all here.”
“…Miss Miyuki.”
“Yes. It's been a long time.”
“You lost a lot of blood and had severe pelvic injuries. We explored your abdomen, stopped the bleeding, and repaired the damage. Your vital signs are stable now.”
“Did you… have to remove something?”
“We didn't perform a hysterectomy. We repaired the damaged areas as well as we could. We'll keep checking how they heal.”
Nanami looked at Sakaguchi, then at Serika. Tears ran down her cheeks.
“…Thank you.”
“Focus on getting better.”
Nanami closed her eyes and took several slow breaths before speaking again.
“It was… Shiozaki.”
The color slowly drained from Serika's face. Asakura said nothing.
“He wouldn't let me leave. Then… he hurt me. I remember begging him to stop before I passed out.”
“When I woke up again… I was here. I don't know where he left me.”
“You don't need to remember the details now. We've treated your injuries, and we'll keep the medical records safe.”
“And Aya, Yayoi, Eri…”
“What happened to them?”
“They're still there. He won't let them go. He forces them to do things they don't want to… Before I got out, none of them had been able to leave.”
Serika rested her hand on the edge of the bed, avoiding Nanami's lines.
“…Are they still alive?”
“When I left… they were all alive.”
Serika raised her other hand to cover her eyes.
“Miss Nanami, you can decide when to tell this, and to whom. For now, rest and continued treatment matter most.”
“…I want to call the police.”
“All right. I'll ask the duty supervisor to contact them now. You don't have to recount everything today, but those three may still be in danger. We can't wait.”
“…Please save them first.”
“I know the old hospital's address and layout. I can provide them if needed.”
“I'll contact the duty supervisor. Let's get this formally reported first.”
Nanami nodded. Asakura brought the tissues from beside the bed and placed them within her reach.
“We'll step outside for a while, then. If you want to see us, just ask a nurse to call us.”
“…Don't go too far, all right?”
“I'll still be in the hospital.”
Nanami watched her for a moment, then finally released the blanket corner she had been gripping.
After leaving the room, Asakura immediately contacted the hospital's duty supervisor and asked them to reach the police. She explained that Nanami had asked to report the crime, and supplied the names of the three other nurses who might still be trapped, along with the old hospital's address.
That afternoon, Serika and Asakura returned to the room. Nanami was awake, watching the light by the window.
“…I've been thinking. There's something I can't keep leaving unsaid.”
“If you're tired, you can tell us next time.”
“It isn't about my condition.”
Nanami turned toward her, tears welling up again.
“Dr. Serika… The things I did back at the hospital…”
“I knew how much you were suffering, but I still took his side. I've owed you an apology all this time.”
“…I haven't forgotten.”
“I know. I wouldn't dare ask you to.”
“I'm sorry. I'm truly… sorry.”
Serika lowered her eyes. A long time passed before she looked up.
“…Back then, there was a lot I didn't dare face either.”
“But that doesn't mean the things you did never happened.”
“…I know.”
Nanami reached out. Serika hesitated, then gently took her hand.
“Thank you… for still being willing to save me.”
“You're a patient. At the operating table, I did what I should.”
Her voice remained calm, but she did not immediately let go of Nanami's hand.
“…There's one more thing. I know it's selfish to say this now.”
“What is it?”
“Could you go back once? To see your father.”
Serika's hand visibly stilled.
“That hospital was supposed to be yours. You can operate, and you care for people. It needs someone who can truly make decisions.”
“…Nanami.”
“I'm not asking you to go back and clean up our mess now. It's just… I used to think everything would be different once you returned.”
Asakura took a step forward, stopping where Nanami could see her.
“Nanami. Let's leave it there for now.”
“…Miss Miyuki?”
“Serika has heard what you wanted her to hear. But some things don't need an answer today.”
“…I'm sorry. I'm rushing things again.”
“I'll think about it. For now, put it out of your mind.”
“…All right.”
Asakura smoothed Nanami's blanket. Serika slowly released her hand and stood up.
“Press the bell if you need anything. A nurse will come, and I'll visit too.”
“…Thank you, Dr. Serika.”
Outside the room, Asakura and Serika stopped beside the automatic doors. The glass reflected their figures in white work clothes.
“…Thank you.”
“I didn't do anything. Just helped her stop talking.”
“I don't only mean today.”
Asakura looked at her. This time, she did not immediately make a joke.
“Back then, too… you were the one who finally got me out.”
“…Serika.”
“But sometimes I remember the things you did to me at the beginning. I know I'm conflicted.”
“Then be conflicted.”
“…What?”
“I did things I shouldn't have. Getting you out afterward wasn't meant to make you owe me gratitude.”
“We're both at Hoshimi now. Someday we can talk properly. But we don't have to say everything today.”
Serika looked toward the end of the corridor and remained silent for a long while.
“…All right. Let's leave it here today.”
“That's exactly what I mean.”
Asakura gave her a small smile and turned toward the nurses' station. Serika stayed where she was until the ward doors had closed behind her again.
