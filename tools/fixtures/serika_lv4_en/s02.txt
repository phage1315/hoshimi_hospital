The emergency room doors stood wide open. Two nurses were wheeling a stretcher into the resuscitation bay.
“An adult woman. A passerby found her in an open area outside town. She was already unresponsive when the ambulance arrived. No identification was found.”
The patient lay beneath a thick blanket, her face pale and her hair plastered to her forehead. The dressing at one side of the bed was soaked through with blood, and a nurse was still applying pressure to control the bleeding.
“Blood pressure?”
“Seventy-eight over forty-six, heart rate one hundred thirty-two. Two IV lines are in place. The blood request has been sent.”
“Notify the blood bank and prepare to transfuse. Continue fluids and monitor her consciousness. Keep her warm.”
A nurse replaced the patient's blanket with a clean, warm one. The numbers on the monitor remained unstable.
“Where was she found?”
“In grass beside the road. The person who found her said she had no clothes or belongings.”
Serika said nothing. Sakaguchi leaned over to assess the patient's breathing, pupils, and abdominal signs, then examined the injury where temporary bleeding control was already in place.
“There's too much bleeding from below. We need to rule out deep abdominal and pelvic injuries first.”
“I'll do a bedside ultrasound.”
“Good. Don't let it delay resuscitation.”
Serika placed the ultrasound probe against the patient's abdomen. The gray-white image on the screen shifted with the probe.
“There's an abnormal collection of fluid in the pelvis. The tissue contours on the right are unclear too… This may be more than a superficial laceration.”
“Traumatic injury?”
“All I can say now is that it's highly suspicious. Her circulation won't let us wait for a full workup.”
“The blood is here.”
Sakaguchi gave the nurse instructions for transfusion and continuous monitoring, then reassessed the bleeding at the injury.
“Pressure is only reducing the bleeding temporarily. We have to locate the deep source.”
Serika returned the probe to its holder and was about to report her other findings. Suddenly, she stopped.
“…Wait.”
She took a step toward the head of the bed, her eyes settling on the patient's profile.
“…It can't be.”
“Dr. Misono?”
Serika's hand remained suspended in midair. Her face had grown paler.
“…Nanami?”
The patient did not respond. Only the heart-rate numbers on the monitor kept changing.
“You know her?”
“Horiuchi… Nanami Horiuchi.”
“A friend?”
Serika parted her lips. A moment passed before any sound came out.
“A former colleague. At my previous hospital…”
“She was my scrub nurse when I operated.”
She stared at Nanami, her gaze fixed rigidly in place.
“Doctor, her blood pressure still isn't coming up.”
“Dr. Misono, look at me.”
At last, Serika looked up.
“She's our patient now. Go and sit down for a moment. I'll take over from here.”
“…”
“You don't have to push yourself. You know who she is. It's natural to be shaken.”
Serika lowered her head and clasped her hands tightly together.
“…Give me one minute.”
She walked to the sink, rinsed her fingers with cold water, and looked up at the mirror. A moment later, she returned to Sakaguchi.
“I can keep working.”
“Are you sure?”
“I know what state I'm in. If you think I'm unfit, I'll step aside.”
“But if you still need an assistant… please let me stay.”
Sakaguchi looked at her. This time, Serika did not look away.
“All right. But I'll judge how you're doing. If necessary, I'll have you step away.”
“Understood.”
Sakaguchi turned toward the nursing desk.
“Ange, notify the OR to prepare for an emergency exploratory laparotomy. We need two scrub nurses and two circulating nurses; the circulating nurses will handle monitoring. Dr. Misono and I will go and prepare.”
“Understood. I'll contact the OR, keep watch over the transfusion, and wait for them to collect the patient.”
Serika looked back at Nanami and reached out to tuck the slipped corner of the blanket back over her.
“…Nanami. Hold on a little longer.”
