After the evening rounds, Asakura clips tomorrow's operating schedule back onto her clipboard and gently caps her pen.
“All right. Ring the bell if you feel unwell tonight. We have a busy morning ahead.”
“Miss Asakura, w-wait!”
“Hmm? Is there something else you forgot to ask, Miss Nikaido?”
Yoriko pulls a small notebook from beside her pillow. Its first two pages are crammed with writing, with several question marks drawn in red.
“…Wow. Are you here as a patient, or to conduct an interview?”
“I'm not trying to dig up any secrets! It's just… tomorrow it really will be my turn to have surgery.”
“I know. You read the consent form twice yesterday, and asked me about it six times today.”
“It wasn't six times! …Huh? You even kept count?”
“A good memory is an asset for a nurse.”
“Then I have one last question… No, maybe a few more.”
“Hehe. Go ahead.”
“From waking up tomorrow morning, to being taken into the operating room, to the doctor actually picking up the knife… what exactly happens to get me ready? Don't skip a single step.”
“Are you sure you want that much detail?”
“Of course. I'm a police officer! The more I know, the more prepared I'll feel.”
“All right. I'll go through it by time. But don't get frightened halfway through and accuse me of scaring you.”
“Don't worry. I'm not that much of a scaredy-cat.”
Asakura pulls up a chair beside the bed and rests the clipboard on her lap. Her expression suggests someone has finally asked about her favorite subject.
“A nurse will wake you at around five thirty tomorrow.”
“Five thirty?! I thought surgery started at nine. Surely I could sleep until seven.”
“Nine is when the incision begins, not when you open your eyes.”
“…Why do you sound so happy explaining that distinction?”
“Because lots of people get it wrong. First we'll check your temperature, blood pressure and pulse, then confirm your wristband, name, procedure, fasting time and allergies.”
“But you already asked all that today!”
“We'll ask again tomorrow. And even more once you reach the surgical area.”
“How worried are you about mistaking me for someone else?”
“Miss Nikaido, when you arrest someone, you can't skip checking their identity just because their face looks familiar, can you?”
“Of course not! …But being asked my name over and over like a suspect still feels strange.”
“Then there's cleaning, skin preparation and putting on your surgical cap. Glasses, jewelry and hair clips are put away, and all your hair goes inside the cap.”
“I can't even wear my glasses? I can't make out your faces without them!”
“Everyone in the operating room wears a cap and mask anyway. You might not recognize us even with your glasses.”
“How is that supposed to be reassuring?!”
“Oh, and your hospital gown and your own clothes all come off. We'll cover you with a blanket afterward. We won't have you going down the corridor like that.”
“…Wait. Did you just say the hospital gown comes off too?”
“Yes. The area needed for surgery has to be cleaned, disinfected and draped. Your own underwear won't be left underneath either.”
“N-not even underwear? That's what I'll be like after I get up tomorrow?”
“We'll keep you covered where you should be. Only the area needed for an examination or preparation will be exposed. It's ordinary preoperative work for us.”
“The more you call it ‘ordinary,’ the more embarrassed I feel…”
“But weren't you the one who said not to skip a single step?”
“…Fine. Go on.”
“Sometime between five and six, we'll have you use the bathroom one last time. After that, you'll need a nurse's help whenever you move. You can't just slip on your slippers and wander out.”
“I'm not a child! …But what if I need the bathroom again later?”
“Tell us. This is a fairly long abdominal operation, so we'll manage urine drainage according to plan. Catheterization comes later.”
“C-catheterization is on the schedule too?!”
“Of course. Were you imagining ‘preoperative preparation’ was something much simpler?”
Yoriko's ears turn red. She starts to close her notebook, hesitates, then opens it again.
“Th-then… will I be awake for that?”
“The current plan is to give you the epidural first, confirm it is working, and then finish that part of the preparation. Tell us whenever you feel anything or are uncomfortable.”
“Oh…”
“What is it? Suddenly stopped taking notes?”
“I can remember that part without writing it down!”
“Around six, we'll recheck the IV, your medical records, images and surgical consent form. All the documentation goes to the operating room with you.”
“And I go along too, right?”
“Of course. Who would the doctor operate on without the patient?”
“I know! I just… wanted to make sure!”
“Then you'll get onto the trolley, with the blanket tucked around you, and a nurse will wheel you over. There'll be another handover at the entrance to the surgical area.”
“I have to lie there while you wheel me all the way? I can't walk?”
“You'll already be prepared for surgery, with lines and coverings to look after. Just lie comfortably and leave the pushing to the nurse.”
“You really intend to keep me lying down from morning until the operation is over…”
“That's how it works. You'll enter the room at seven. The operating table is a little narrower and firmer than this bed.”
“How narrow?”
“Narrow enough for the doctors to work from both sides. We'll put down padding and position your head, shoulders, arms and legs.”
“That sounds like arranging a piece of… very important evidence.”
“Hehe, you've finally found a police comparison. But evidence doesn't suddenly sneeze by itself. We still have to tell you how to help us.”
“Am I really that unreliable?”
“You're a very talkative patient. But when we change your position, it's best to wait for instructions. Several nurses will count ‘one, two, three’ together before moving you.”
“…I don't even get to decide where I lie.”
“Because you'll be undergoing surgical preparation. You can tell us if you're uncomfortable, but you can't roll over and settle yourself into another position.”
“You won't let me fall, will you?”
“There'll be safety straps, padding and supports. They're there to prevent sliding and pressure injuries, not to tie you to a rack like luggage.”
“Don't you think that last comparison made it scarier than saying nothing?”
“Would you prefer the medical terminology?”
“…Never mind.”
“Once you're on the table, we'll put ECG electrodes on your chest, a blood-pressure cuff around your arm, a pulse-oximeter probe on your finger, and secure the IV line safely. Someone in the room will keep watching the monitors.”
“So by then I'll have a whole pile of things attached to me?”
“Yes. Everyone is working to keep you safe during surgery.”
“…Miss Asakura, every time you say ‘safe,’ another thing I've never heard of turns up.”
Asakura's eyes curve with her smile, as though Yoriko's discovery has amused her.
“Sometime after seven, Dr. Nanjo will check everything with you, then administer the epidural.”
“That's… the one with a needle in your back and a tube?”
“Yes. You'll need to lie on your side or sit up, then hold that position. The doctor will explain before doing anything.”
“It's my abdomen that needs surgery. Why do I get a needle in my back first…?”
“Because anesthesia blocks sensation carried by the nerves. It doesn't always go directly into the incision site. Afterward, we'll test the area of numbness. Surgery can only start once it's sufficient.”
“So I won't just close my eyes, sleep, and wake up when it's finished?”
“Not according to this plan. Generally, you'll remain awake. Dr. Nanjo will keep monitoring you and adjust the plan if needed. Tell us immediately about any actual sharp pain. If the block is insufficient, we'll address it first, not ask you to endure it.”
“…I'll be awake the whole time, knowing what everyone is doing to me?”
“You'll be aware of quite a lot. Once the drapes are in place, you won't see the work on your abdomen, but you'll hear some sounds.”
“…Sounds?”
“Instrument packs being opened, metal instruments placed on trays, nurses checking things with one another. Someone reporting readings, someone asking about the light, someone checking the suction.”
“S-stop a moment! I feel like I can hear it already!”
Asakura really does stop and watches her quietly. Yoriko covers her ears, then slowly lowers her hands two seconds later.
“…And then?”
“Huh? Didn't you just say stop?”
“I meant pause! Not skip everything that comes afterward!”
“I see. You're the sort who covers her eyes during a horror movie, then peeks through her fingers.”
“This isn't a movie! I'm the one who'll actually be lying there tomorrow!”
“Exactly. That's why I'm explaining it so carefully.”
“You don't… enjoy talking about this a little, do you?”
“I do. Getting the people, instruments, anesthesia, timing and positions all properly organized is one of the interesting parts of an operation.”
“Interesting for you. For me…”
“You'll be the center of attention in the whole operating room tomorrow.”
“The center of attention?! Don't make it sound like the star is making her entrance!”
“But it's true. Dr. Sakaguchi, the surgeon; Dr. Nanjo, handling anesthesia; Miss Nakai, handling instruments; and the circulating nurse… everyone will be preparing for your operation.”
“…An entire room of people, all bustling around me?”
“Yes. You can leave it to us with confidence.”
“How do you make the scariest sentence sound like a compliment?!”
“After seven thirty, there'll be catheterization, abdominal disinfection, small adjustments to your position and various other preparations. The nurses will uncover the relevant area as needed, then cover it again.”
“How big an area will you disinfect?”
“Larger than the eventual incision. Your lower abdomen needs thorough disinfection. The solution may feel a little cool, though after anesthesia you might not feel it the way you normally would.”
“…So you'll lift the blanket off me, then look closely at my abdomen while getting things ready.”
“Of course. We have to check the disinfected area, your position, the skin and where the lines are. It's our work.”
“You keep saying ‘work.’ I'll be lying there without any clothes while a whole group gathers around…”
“Because it really is ordinary for us. Nobody is coming in specially to admire how frightened Miss Nikaido looks.”
“Waaah… You say that so naturally, it makes me even more embarrassed!”
“Oh? Why?”
“Because I'm the only one who thinks this is a huge deal! You're all just thinking, ‘Right, next item!’”
“Then tomorrow you'll see for yourself just how dependable our ‘next item’ is.”
“…Is that meant to reassure me, or warn me what's coming?”
“A little of both.”
Asakura deliberately imitates Yoriko's earlier hesitant tone and laughs. Yoriko puffs out her cheeks, but has no reply.
“We'll start placing sterile drapes around eight. The surgical area that needs to be exposed stays in the center opening; everything else is covered. Afterward, you'll hardly be able to see any of the work around your abdomen.”
“So I'll hear you talking, but I won't see what you're doing?”
“Most of the time. Close your eyes if you like, or look at the surgical lights. There's no rule that patients must keep their eyes open to watch the excitement.”
“But if I can't see, I might keep guessing!”
“You can ask. We'll explain the steps we can tell you about.”
“What if I'm cold?”
“Say you're cold.”
“What if my arm feels uncomfortable where it's secured?”
“Tell us it feels uncomfortable. We'll check whether we can adjust it.”
“What if I want to sit up and look?”
“Tell us first. Don't get up by yourself. You may already have lines connected and anesthesia in effect.”
“What if I suddenly decide I don't want the operation?”
“If you want to change your decision, tell the doctor immediately so we can confirm it again. Being frightened and changing your decision aren't the same thing. Don't hide either.”
“…And what if I just see the scalpel and suddenly want to run back to my room?”
“At least tell us first. We can help with your nerves, but we can't guess every escape plan before you say anything.”
“You just guessed a whole bunch of my questions!”
“That's because you're really very easy to read, Miss Nikaido.”
“Around eight thirty, Miss Nakai will count the instruments and gauze again, while the doctors check the images to confirm the locations of the fibroids being treated.”
“…Several of them.”
“Yes. This isn't a matter of removing one little thing and finishing. The surgeon will deal with the fibroids in each location, then carefully repair the uterine wall. It will take longer than an ordinary minor operation.”
“When you say ‘each location,’ I suddenly feel like there's a whole to-do list inside my abdomen.”
“That's a good description. We really do have to check the preoperative images and surgical plan item by item.”
“But I'm a person. Why do you make me sound like a complicated investigation…?”
“You can think of it as us solving a very troublesome case for you. But this time you're the most important witness, so you need to lie quietly.”
“…I never knew being a witness was such hard work.”
“Next, Dr. Nanjo will make a final check of the block, and Dr. Sakaguchi will prepare additional local infiltration at the incision site. Everyone will complete the checks before surgery begins.”
“But I'll already have an epidural! Why inject the incision site too?”
“It's a supplement. Sensation around the incision isn't quite the same as pulling sensations deep inside. Dr. Nanjo will continue assessing you and only let Dr. Sakaguchi start once the anesthesia is sufficient.”
“…Will I hear them all say my name?”
“Yes. Everyone must confirm the patient, procedure, site, anesthetic status and anything needing special attention. You'll notice the whole room suddenly quiet down to listen carefully to each person's response.”
“I'm already imagining what that sounds like…”
“At around nine, the surgeon confirms that preparation is complete, the assistant takes position, and Miss Nakai passes over the first scalpel. Then—”
“Stop! Stop! Don't say it yet!”
Yoriko yanks up the blanket so abruptly that her glasses tilt. Asakura really does fall silent, and considerately waits for her to straighten them.
“Didn't you say you wanted to hear everything up to the incision?”
“I do! But you make it sound… far too real!”
“Because tomorrow it will be real.”
“Don't say that with such a happy face!”
“Then I can continue from ‘Miss Nakai passes over the first scalpel.’”
“N-no!”
“Hehe. You really are the kind who gets to the scariest part of a horror movie and still can't bear to turn off the television.”
“I keep telling you, this is my operation, not a horror movie!!”
“All right. I won't go any further for now.”
She closes the operating schedule. The room falls quiet, and Yoriko finds herself looking at her notebook again.
“…But is there anything you haven't told me?”
“Huh? Didn't you already tell me to stop?”
“I just want to make sure! The more I hear, the scarier it gets, but if I don't know, my imagination makes it even worse.”
“I see. Usually you're the one finding out about other people, but today other people know more about what's happening to you than you do.”
“Exactly! The whole operating room knows what they're going to do with me next. I'm the only one lying there, unable to do anything…”
“Then let us do the work. Your job is to tell us what's uncomfortable and cooperate when we need you to.”
“So you're in charge of preparation, disinfection, anesthesia and cutting… and I'm in charge of getting cut?”
“You could use the nicer expression: ‘undergoing surgery.’”
“But from the patient's point of view, isn't getting cut exactly my job?”
“From the patient's point of view… yes, I suppose it is.”
“At least pretend to disagree!”
Asakura finally laughs aloud. Yoriko laughs with her, though she is still clutching the corner of the blanket tightly.
“Then tomorrow, if I'm really too frightened to say anything… will you tell me which step you're at?”
“Yes. And we'll tell you in advance when we need you to cooperate.”
“Even the anesthesia, catheterization, disinfection and draping?”
“We'll tell you everything we can. If there's something you'd rather not hear, you can ask me to hold off.”
“One more thing. Don't suddenly say ‘All done’ tomorrow.”
“Why?”
“Because every time you say ‘All done,’ it means you're about to do something even scarier!”
“Then I'll be more precise. ‘Epidural complete,’ ‘Disinfection complete,’ ‘Draping complete,’ ‘Final checks complete’…”
“…See?! That's even scarier!”
“At least it's precise.”
“Do all you nurses enjoy explaining the things people fear, step by step?”
“Of course. That's our job. Besides, you're asking so earnestly that I'd feel bad leaving anything out.”
“I'll probably dream about the operating table tonight.”
“Think of it as getting familiar with the surroundings. Get some sleep, Miss Nikaido.”
“…If I get scared again tomorrow, don't complain that I ask too many questions.”
“When have I ever complained? Ask whatever you like. But when we say ‘start’ tomorrow, I won't make the doctor pause until you've finished your notes.”
“I'm not actually going to take notes on the operating table!”
“Right. We'll wake you at five thirty, put you on the table at seven, drape you at eight, and start at nine. We'll remember every step for you.”
Asakura stands and walks to the door, still wearing that professional, deeply satisfied smile.
“…Now I think the scariest part is that you really do remember everything.”
The door closes softly. Yoriko switches off the bedside lamp, her notebook already tucked beside her pillow. A long time later, she feels for it again and opens it to the page marked “nine o'clock.”
