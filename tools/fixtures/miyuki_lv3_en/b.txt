The plaza is much busier on Sunday than on weekdays. Sakaguchi turns his phone over on the bench and looks up once more at the people passing by.
He and Miyuki Asakura agreed to meet at two in the afternoon. Now the minute hand on the plaza clock has leisurely completed another lap.
“…I hope nothing’s happened to her.”
Sakaguchi calls again. The phone rings for a long time before someone finally answers.
“…Hello?”
“Asakura? Where are you now?”
“…Mm… Dr. Sakaguchi?”
“Yes, it’s me. Are you all right?”
“…………”
Rustling comes through the phone, followed by the dull thud of something falling onto the floor.
“Aaah—!!!”
“What happened?!”
“Oh no!! I overslept!!”
“…Overslept?”
“Is today Sunday?! Were we supposed to meet at two?!”
“Yes. It’s ten past three now.”
“Th-three?!”
The other end falls silent for two seconds, then hurried footsteps follow.
“Are you still waiting for me?!”
“I am.”
“I’m sorry! I’m so sorry! I’ll be right there!”
“Don’t rush. Were you on duty last night?”
“No.”
“Then how did you sleep until now?”
“It’s that drama I watched yesterday! The last episode ended on such an outrageous cliffhanger. How could I possibly stop there!”
“…So you watched all night?”
“Until sunrise. Then I thought I’d sleep for a little while… The next thing I knew, your call woke me.”
“Quite a ‘little while.’”
“Don’t laugh! I’ll be there in twenty… no, thirty minutes!”
“Take your time. Just make sure you put your shoes on properly.”
“Dr. Sakaguchi!!”
The call ends. Sakaguchi looks at the dark screen, then up at the three-o’clock afternoon sky.
“…Asakura notices even a handover five minutes early at the hospital, and yet…”
Forty minutes later, Asakura jogs over from the far side of the plaza. She is in light casual clothes with a small bag over her shoulder, her hair still showing the curves of a hurried styling.
“Dr. Sakaguchi—!”
“You finally made it.”
“I’m sorry…”
She presses her palms together, smiling sweetly and obediently. Before Sakaguchi can speak, Asakura raises her phone.
“I promise, I really did set an alarm.”
“For what time?”
“…Eleven.”
“Then why did you only wake up at three?”
“I’d like to know too. My phone says I turned it off, but I don’t remember doing it at all.”
“You remember the hospital rota so clearly, but not turning off your own alarm?”
“That’s different. Patients are waiting at the hospital. Of course I can’t be careless.”
“On a day off… nobody needs surgery just because I stay in bed a little longer.”
“Someone waited for you for over an hour today.”
“Ooh… I admit that was my fault. What would it take for you to forgive me, Dr. Sakaguchi?”
“Let’s at least get something to eat first. I’m getting hungry.”
“All right! My treat today. You can have anything you like!”
“Really?”
“Well… except the kind of fancy restaurant you have to book three months ahead.”
“Quite a few conditions attached.”
“I haven’t even had breakfast yet. Have mercy on me first.”
She gently tugs Sakaguchi’s sleeve and heads toward the shopping street. Her embarrassment doesn’t last long; there is already a spring in her step again.
They enter a small restaurant still serving afternoon tea. Their window seat overlooks the pedestrians outside, and patterned paper menus lie on the table.
“Huh… They’ve stopped serving the lunch set.”
“It’s almost four.”
“I know. But I haven’t had lunch today.”
“So your lunchtime is nothing like the hospital’s.”
“It’s a rare day off. The clock ought to work around me.”
“I’ve never heard you say that at the hospital.”
“How could I? If a patient is due at seven, it means seven. If an instrument is missing, can I say I’ll look for it after I’ve had my sleep?”
“So the moment you finish work, you become a completely different person?”
“Not a different person. This person finally gets to be lazy.”
Asakura orders a hot sandwich, then studies the dessert section intently.
“Didn’t you say you needed a meal? Why are you still looking at cake?”
“The meal is for now. The cake is for afterward. Surely a doctor understands the order?”
“I thought you might have some special diet plan.”
“I do. Step one: feed the me who’s been hungry for half a day.”
“And step two?”
“Make sure the handsome doctor who waited an hour for me enjoys his meal too.”
“…Are you trying to sweet-talk me?”
“Can’t I? I’m very good at reassuring patients.”
“So I’m a patient today?”
“If you’d like, I could take care of you until you feel better.”
“Something about that doesn’t sound quite right.”
“Hehe. Let’s eat first, then. No more hospital talk.”
The hot sandwich arrives. Asakura closes her eyes in satisfaction at the first bite, all her clever remarks momentarily defeated by food.
“Good?”
“Mm! Try some. This sauce is delicious.”
“You were late, but now you sound like you’re showing me your favorite places.”
“Of course. Being late means I have to make the rest of our time even more worthwhile.”
After they eat, sunlight slants across the table. Asakura taps her dessert glass with a small spoon and finally stops rushing to speak.
“When I watch you work, you really don’t seem like a newcomer.”
“This again? Didn’t I tell you last time that I’m only new to Hoshimi?”
“I mean, you seem to know all about handovers, operating-room routines, and exactly where newcomers will make mistakes.”
“Well… I’ve done all those things before.”
“Just how much were you responsible for?”
“Routine nursing, scheduling, training new staff… Later I was even the leader of the second nursing team.”
“Team leader?”
“Officially. I led a whole team on shift, and before leaving I had to make sure everyone had handed everything over properly.”
“Then why have you kept calling yourself an inexperienced new nurse since coming to Hoshimi?”
“Because I really am unfamiliar with the people and systems here. The way my old hospital scheduled things doesn’t mean Hoshimi does the same.”
“That sounds quite conscientious.”
“If you think that means I’m going to become meek and obedient, you’re wrong.”
“I wouldn’t dare think that now.”
“When I was promoted, everyone said it was a good thing. I could manage more people, and my words carried more weight.”
“At first, I was happy too. Who doesn’t like having their work recognized?”
“And then?”
“Then I found that everyone wanted me on their side.”
“What should have been arranging nursing care for patients turned into guessing which superior might be upset first.”
“You didn’t like that kind of work?”
“I don’t mind managing things. Who should help when someone is overwhelmed; how to teach a newcomer until she understands—I like all that.”
“But smiling to appease people, finding out who’s fighting with whom, cleaning up messes for the higher-ups that have nothing to do with nursing… No, thank you.”
“Did you say that outright back then too?”
“Yes. Though whenever I did, someone would say I was immature.”
“You don’t look as though that would bother you now.”
“It does. But one day I suddenly thought: why should those people get to decide whether I’m happy every day?”
Asakura scoops up a small spoonful of dessert, as though discussing something she has already thought through thoroughly.
“I want to care for patients and teach newcomers. Those things interest me, so I’ll keep doing them.”
“I don’t want to take sides all day, or have dinner with people I dislike every evening after work. So I won’t be that kind of team leader anymore.”
“So coming to Hoshimi was your own choice?”
“Of course. You didn’t think I came here for retraining because I couldn’t do anything right at my old hospital, did you?”
“I never said that.”
“Good. Otherwise I might have to check your handover sheet again tomorrow.”
“Hey, weren’t we avoiding work talk today?”
“Oh, right. I nearly brought the hospital into our date again.”
“But you really do seem happy now.”
“I am. I can work hard, sleep in, watch dramas, and dress up to go out for good food.”
“I’m only twenty-three. Surely there’s more to look forward to than shifts and rotas?”
“Fair enough. What else would you like to do?”
“So much. Go to the seaside, see a new film, sleep until I wake up naturally…”
“You already managed the last one today.”
“And find a man I like the look of to take me on a date now and then.”
“…Oh.”
“Hmm? Such a mild reaction?”
“You said it so naturally.”
“It is perfectly natural.”
“Rather than flatter those awful, powerful doctors, I’d much rather tease a handsome doctor I actually like.”
“…You wouldn’t happen to mean me, would you?”
“Hehe. What do you think?”
She doesn’t rush to say anything else, merely sets her spoon back on the saucer and smiles at Sakaguchi.
“You’re being deliberately vague.”
“It took me so long to get a whole day with you. Of course I have to make it interesting.”
“Didn’t you only remember this date at three?”
“…You’re awful. Can we retire that joke now?”
“Who just said she wanted to make today interesting?”
“Hmm… So Dr. Sakaguchi can talk back after all.”
Asakura smiles and reaches for the bill. Before Sakaguchi can say anything more, she is already on her way to the till.
“Come on. The afternoon isn’t over yet.”
Leaving the restaurant, they follow the shopping street to the river. A breeze blows along the waterside path, where the Sunday crowds are much thinner than in the shops.
“You said you like teaching newcomers. But sometimes you’re quite strict with them.”
“Am I? I thought I’d been exceptionally gentle here at Hoshimi.”
“Sometimes you even seem a little pleased to see someone nervous.”
“You caught me?”
“You admitted that far too quickly.”
“Being nervous isn’t always bad. Newcomers ask me serious questions because they know how much they don’t understand.”
“Patients too. Some are scared, but still desperately want to hear what will happen next.”
“…Do you mean that policewoman from before?”
“Hehe. She’s not the only one.”
“You said we wouldn’t talk about the hospital today.”
“This is a personal interest, not a discussion about the rota. Two different things.”
“That sounds a little dangerous.”
“Shall I tell you something even more dangerous?”
“Let’s hear it.”
Asakura turns to face Sakaguchi and takes two steps backward. As if about to offer a serious professional opinion, she suddenly lowers her voice.
“I’ve always thought surgery is actually like an enormous SM play session.”
“…………What?”
“Think about it. Outside, people can walk, sit, and make their own decisions. Once they enter the operating room, so much has to be left to the doctors and nurses.”
“…That’s true.”
“Where they lie, where their hands and feet go, when they can move… It’s their own body, but we set the pace for everything that follows.”
“That’s for the operation.”
“I know. But don’t you think that mixture of fear, embarrassment, and having to trust someone else… is rather intriguing?”
“I’ve never heard a nurse describe her work that way before.”
“Hehe. That just means the others hide it better.”
“You don’t actually treat patients as playthings, do you?”
“Of course I do my job properly at work. I wouldn’t deliberately disrupt an operation just because I found something amusing.”
“But observing a patient’s reactions is part of a nurse’s skill. Terrified, yet unable to stop asking what comes next—sometimes they’re very easy to read.”
“So you just smile and wait for them to keep asking?”
“If they want to know, I can hardly rush to force the answers on them.”
“…No wonder you were so pleased that day.”
“Still, not every patient feels that way.”
“In all the operations I’ve assisted with, quite a few young women have been both frightened and curious, always wondering what we’ll do next.”
“It doesn’t seem quite as obvious with older patients.”
“…You really do pay close attention.”
“I am a nurse, after all. Working out what a patient is actually worried about is part of the job.”
She smiles and turns forward again, casually brushing the windblown hair away from her cheek.
“Come to think of it… for some reason, almost all the patients at our hospital are young women.”
“…………”
“Dr. Sakaguchi, don’t you think that’s a little strange?”
“I wouldn’t know how to answer that even if you asked me.”
“Hehehe. Very strange.”
Asakura walks on as though nothing happened, leaving Sakaguchi standing there wondering what exactly he has just been asked.
The light gradually softens. They sit on a bench beside the river. Someone cycles past in the distance, and the bell soon fades into the breeze.
“I can understand now why you don’t want to deal with factions and positions anymore.”
“So serious all of a sudden?”
“You’re clearly better at managing people than many others. But from what you’ve said, you seem to know exactly what you don’t want.”
“It took me some time to work that out.”
“You and Serika used to work at the same hospital, didn’t you?”
“…Yes.”
“You came to Hoshimi together. Did something happen?”
Asakura gazes at the river, slowly gathering up the strap of the small bag beside her. She doesn’t answer right away.
“Dr. Sakaguchi.”
“Yes?”
“Let’s leave what happened there, there.”
“…Sorry. I shouldn’t have asked so suddenly.”
“You don’t need to apologize. I know you’re curious.”
“There are just some things I don’t want to talk about yet.”
She turns back, still smiling, but this time she isn’t teasing him.
“If you want to get to know me… could you start with who I am now?”
“Of course.”
“That easily?”
“If you don’t want to talk, I won’t ask.”
“…Thank you.”
The distant river reflects the sunset. Asakura looks away for a moment, then turns back to Sakaguchi as though something has occurred to her.
“There is one thing I’d like to ask you, though.”
“What is it?”
“When you can, would you help Serika a little more?”
“Serika?”
“Sometimes she struggles to say what she wants to say. You’ve probably noticed that working with her.”
“…A little.”
“I can’t tell you those things for her. But if one day she wants to tell you herself… would you hear her out?”
“Yes. I’ll remember.”
“Then I’m counting on you.”
She exhales softly, as though she has finally entrusted him with a small concern that has been on her mind.
“All right—! That’s enough serious talk for today!”
“You switch gears quickly.”
“It’s a rare chance to go on a date. I don’t want the whole thing to feel like a nursing meeting.”
“What would you like to do next?”
“Hmm… First, let’s see if that dessert shop is still open.”
“Didn’t you just have cake?”
“That was afternoon tea. It’s almost dinnertime now.”
“Your sense of time really does follow its own rules.”
“At the hospital, my time belongs to the patients and everyone else. I want to keep the rest of today for myself.”
“…Fair enough.”
“And you.”
“Huh?”
“Why else would I have rushed over today? Just to eat a sandwich on my own?”
“You make it sound as if you weren’t late.”
“Oh, Sakaguchi-kun. Could we stop bringing that up for the rest of today?”
Her “Sakaguchi-kun” comes lightly and naturally. As Sakaguchi turns, Asakura is already standing, holding out her hand.
“…All right. I’ll let you off today.”
“Hehe. You really are a good doctor.”
“What does being a doctor have to do with it?”
“You waited for me, listened to me, and will even join me for a second round of dessert. Very high marks.”
“You have some strange criteria.”
“You only just noticed?”
Sakaguchi takes her outstretched hand, and they walk along the path toward the shopping street as the streetlights come on.
“Oh, and I’ll definitely be on time for our next date.”
“What time will you set your alarm?”
“…Why don’t you call to wake me up?”
“Wouldn’t that be just like today?”
“It’s different. Today I didn’t hear your good morning until three.”
“Next time… I’d like to hear it earlier.”
She smiles, but doesn’t let go of his hand. Warm lights come on in the shop windows ahead.
